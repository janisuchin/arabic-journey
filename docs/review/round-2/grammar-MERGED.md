# grammar: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: I read the whole grammar chunk (78 grammar cards, about 3,060 lines): every Arabic line, harakat and case ending, formula, table cell, explanation, example, translation, transliteration and tap gloss. The Arabic itself is clean: I found no misspellings, no wrong case endings after inna/kāna/laysa/lam/lan/an, no agreement or conjugation errors, and the rules for -ūna/-īna, the numbers 3–10 vs 11–99, idāfa, hollow verbs, imperatives, the passive and conditionals are correct. There are 30 errors (9 major, 21 minor): 2 grammar-fact (the dual -ayni said to follow 'a verb', major; '11 upwards' for -atan, minor), 1 arabic (أَدَاة without the construct vowel, read as 'Adāh'), 24 gloss and 3 engine. The main pattern is tap glosses chosen from another sense or another day: wrong person on ambiguous ta- verbs (تَشْرَبُ 'you' in 'she drinks', تُحِبُّ, تَتَّصِلَ, تَكُونُ), مَا = 'what' throughout the mā-negation lesson, إِنَّ = 'that (after qāla)' throughout the 'Indeed: inna' lesson, أَفْعَلَ = 'I do' for the Form IV pattern, active verbs glossed as passive in the passive lesson, comparative/superlative swapped (أَحْسَنُ = 'the best' in 'books are better'), كَرِيم = 'Karim' in رَمَضَان كَرِيم and السَّاعَةَ = 'at the hour' for 'this clock'. Engine: the new s·h separator now fires after a hyphen ('-·hu'), tanwīn before al- lacks the helping i ('mutaʾakhkhiran l-layla'), and unvocalised words and ؟ in prose are dropped or changed. Tap glosses: I checked all 3,178 taps (701 tap lines); about 125 were wrong or misleading for their line (the largest groups: إِنَّ 18, passive forms 15, لَوْ in لَوْ سَمَحْتَ 13, مَا 10, endings named as letters 9, wrong person 8, superlative/comparative 7).
