# Culture chapter `food` (cul.food, n 5) - open points for a teacher

File: content/culture/food.json (status needs-review). Photos: assets/culture/food-1..4.jpg.

1. Section titles in Arabic: الطَّعَامُ وَالْمَائِدَة (chapter), الْمُقَبِّلَات for "mezze" - check this is the best MSA term (vs. الْمَازَة / الْمَزَّة); body says Levantine speech uses "mazza".
2. صَحْتَيْن marked as Levantine spoken (not MSA). I did NOT claim it for Egypt; please confirm the regional label and whether Egypt/Gulf use it. The usual Levantine reply ("ʿa-albak") is not included; add it if wanted.
3. هَنِيئًا مَرِيئًا given as the "classical" wish; glosses of مَرِيئًا ("may it be wholesome") - check wording.
4. Egyptian عَيْش: stated that in MSA the word means "living, life". Fine?
5. Fact claims to verify: state-subsidised bread "in several countries" (Egypt, Jordan…); Iraqi tannour bread; Moroccan thicker round loaf; older people kissing dropped bread; couscous on Friday in Morocco; machboos in Bahrain/Kuwait/Qatar; ful also eaten in Sudan and the Levant; mansaf also eaten in Palestine, often over shrak bread.
6. Right hand described as "a custom that comes from Islamic tradition" - check tone is neutral enough.
7. Halal paragraph: "Muslims do not eat pork, so pork is rare or absent on most menus" - deliberately general; some countries (e.g. Lebanon, Egypt for non-Muslims) do sell pork. Consider adding a nuance about Christian communities / alcohol if wanted.
8. Sentence fods7 "الْمَنْسَفُ أَشْهَرُ طَبَقٍ فِي الْأُرْدُنّ" - superlative + indefinite genitive; OK at level 5?
9. Reading: "فِي صِينِيَّةٍ كَبِيرَة" ("on a big tray") - Jordanians often say سِدْر for the mansaf tray; kept MSA صِينِيَّة.
10. Photo food-1 (hero) is captioned as a Syrian mezze plate per the Commons description (Al-Husn, Homs).
