# cul.desert (n 6) — open points for a teacher

- Vowelling style: case endings inside sentences, pause form on the last word (as in day 60). Names سِيوَة, رَمّ, الْأَحْسَاء left without a final case vowel.
- وَادِي رَمّ: vowelled ramm (Wādī Ramm). Check the preferred vowelling.
- dess9 أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا، تَفَضَّلْ وَاشْرَبِ الْقَهْوَة: natural as a written model? Many hosts would just say تَفَضَّلْ with the cup.
- dess8 مَصْنُوعٌ مِنْ شَعْرِ الْمَاعِز: الْمَاعِز glossed "the goats" (collective). Check.
- Facts to verify:
  - Rub' al-Khali dunes "more than 200 metres high" (sources give up to about 250 m).
  - Summer shade temperature "often above 40 degrees" (stated as general, not for one place).
  - Al-Ahsa: "well over two million date palms"; UNESCO World Heritage 2018. Wadi Rum: UNESCO (Wadi Rum Protected Area, 2011).
  - Siwa: Siwi (Berber) spoken at home by many people.
  - Arabic coffee on UNESCO intangible heritage list 2015 (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Oman, Qatar).
  - Canopus (suhayl) rising as the sign of the end of the worst summer heat in Arabia (regional custom, mainly Gulf / Najd).
  - Star names: Aldebaran from ad-dabarān "the follower"; Altair from (an-nasr) aṭ-ṭāʾir "the flying (eagle)".
  - "Three days" guest custom: described as "in many tribes", not universal.
- Camel words: kept to جَمَل, نَاقَة, إِبِل; the "thousand words" claim is called an exaggeration. Check the tone.
- Tent: goat-hair weave swelling in rain and the guest/family curtain are described as traditional. The photo shows tourist-camp tents in that style (said in the caption).
- Hero photo: one camel in Wadi Rum (no people). Siwa photo: a lake with palm groves.
