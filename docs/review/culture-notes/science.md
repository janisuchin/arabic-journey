# science (cul.science, n=8) — open points for a teacher

- House of Wisdom: the text says historians still debate its size and role (al-Ma'mun, r. 813-833). Check the tone is right.
- Reading says al-Khwarizmi "worked there" (هُنَاكَ عَمِلَ الْخُوَارِزْمِيُّ) in the House of Wisdom: the traditional account, not proven in detail. Soften if the reviewer prefers.
- Fatima al-Fihri: Arabic uses the feminine nisba فَاطِمَةُ الْفِهْرِيَّةُ; the English uses "al-Fihri", as most English sources do. The 859 founding date is traditional. "Oldest university still working" is worded as "often described as". The library reopened in 2016: please confirm.
- Paper in Baghdad "by the end of the 8th century" (the usual date is about 794-795).
- Dates: Ibn al-Haytham c. 965-c. 1040 (worded "about"); Ibn Sina 980-1037.
- Etymologies follow the usual dictionary accounts. Sugar and lemon reached English through Arabic but are older (Sanskrit and Persian); the text says "comes from", which could be softened to "came through". Admiral = amir + al-, explained with أَمِيرُ الْبَحْر. Magazine comes from the plural makhazin through Italian magazzino; the card shows only the singular مَخْزَن.
- Eye photo (science-2, "Cheshm manuscript.jpg"): the Commons page has no description. It looks like the well-known eye diagram from a manuscript of Hunayn ibn Ishaq's book on the eye (copy c. 1200). The caption is kept generic because of this. Please verify before naming it.
- Hero astrolabe is Persian (18th century) and the caption says so. It could be swapped for an Arab-made one if wanted.
- Grammar ids: صَارَ (scis5) is tagged "kana" as a sister of kana. The relative clause in scis4 (عَالِمًا عَاشَ, no relative pronoun after an indefinite noun) has no grammar id of its own.
- scis2 mistake (verb-subject agreement) was not added; the only other mistake left is on scis3 (kana + accusative).
