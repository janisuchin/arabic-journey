# ramadan (cul.ramadan, n=2) — open points for a teacher

- Reading: the passage uses plural verbs (يَأْكُلُونَ, يُفْطِرُونَ) after the singular collective عَائِلَةُ سَلْمَى; check whether a teacher prefers feminine singular throughout.
- rams3 / body: spelling اللّٰه / اللّٰهُ with superscript alif, as in the course vocab note. Check that TTS reads it correctly.
- rams6 نُفْطِرُ عَلَى تَمْرٍ وَمَاء: "break the fast on/with" uses عَلَى. Common and idiomatic, but confirm.
- rams9 quotes the greetings without quotation marks, because the colon and quote marks may not be accepted as punctuation tokens. A teacher may want «…» if the app supports them.
- rams12 mistake uses an em dash between greeting and reply in the wrong/right fields. These are display text only.
- Facts to confirm: Eid al-Fitr is "usually three days" (public holidays vary by country); Eid al-Adha is "about seventy days" after Eid al-Fitr (10 Dhu al-Hijja). The chapter only says that the fanous custom is "linked to medieval Cairo" (the Fatimid origin stories differ). It says that Levantine Christians bake ka'k/ma'moul for Easter.
- Daytime eating rules for visitors are described in general terms only ("against the law or strongly frowned upon in some places, restaurants open as usual in others, rules have changed"). No country is named on purpose, because laws change (e.g. the Gulf states in recent years).
- "free iftar tables in the street" refers to the Egyptian mawa'id ar-rahman and similar customs elsewhere. The Arabic term is left out to avoid a gloss gap.
- Tarawih is named in English only (no Arabic run).
- Card الْحَجّ is glossed "the Hajj, the pilgrimage". The body calls Dhu al-Hijja "the month of الْحَجّ (al-ḥajj)", which simplifies the name ذُو الْحِجَّة. A teacher may want the full month name.
