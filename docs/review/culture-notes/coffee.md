# coffee (cul.coffee) — open points for a teacher

- Etymology of "ahlan wa-sahlan" given as "often explained as: you have come to family and to easy, level ground" — a folk/common explanation; check wording.
- Gulf coffee: "beans often only lightly roasted", spices cardamom (+ saffron, cloves, rose water in some families); Jordanian Bedouin darker brew mentioned in English only. Check that this regional framing is fair.
- Serving etiquette: pot in the left hand, cup offered with the right (Gulf) — commonly reported; confirm.
- Cup shaking (hazz al-finjan) presented as a Gulf / Bedouin custom, explicitly not universal (Cairo, Beirut, Casablanca named as places where many do not use it). Confirm.
- "Many guests drink one, two or three cups" — deliberately avoided the "odd number / three cups" rule, which varies.
- UNESCO: "Arabic coffee, a symbol of generosity", inscribed 2015 (Representative List; UAE, Saudi Arabia, Oman, Qatar). Used in body and quiz q6 — please verify.
- Tea: Egypt/Levant strong sweet black tea in small glasses; Morocco/Algeria/Tunisia green mint tea poured from height. Generalisations kept to "often".
- Refuse-then-accept rhythm and "hand on your heart" gesture when declining — common but varies.
- Arabic: طَيِّب used as "all right, OK" (MSA-acceptable, also colloquial). تَفَضَّلْ glossed as verb (to a man); feminine تَفَضَّلِي not taught here.
- Reading: خَالِد written in pause form before colons and at sentence end, خَالِدٌ mid-sentence; سَامِي uncased.
- Word card note for أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا mentions the reply أَهْلًا بِكَ — passed the checker (بِكَ is known to the course glossary); check it is shown/tappable.
- Photos: coffee-2 has a faint reflection of the photographer in the pot; coffee-3 shows a tea glass next to strings of beads (possibly prayer beads) — neutral, but replace if unwanted. coffee-4 is a museum display (Coffee Museum, Dubai), not a lived-in majlis.
