# souq (cul.souq, n=4) - open points for a teacher

- suqs9 / words / reading: "مَا آخِرُ سِعْرٍ؟" for "what's your last price?" - idafa with an indefinite genitive. Check that it sounds natural in MSA (spoken alternatives: akhir kalam, akhir si'r).
- suqs11: "يُغْلِقُ بَعْضُ الْبَاعَةِ مَحَلَّاتِهِمْ" - masculine verb agreeing with بَعْض; check.
- suqs12 / section title: "الْمَرْكَز التِّجَارِيّ" for mall; many speakers just say mool. Check the wording.
- Gender of سُوق: masculine is used throughout (قَدِيمٌ in suqs2), following the course vocabulary note (both genders are heard).
- مُسَاوَمَة is used as the MSA word for bargaining; colloquial words (fisal in Egypt, etc.) are not mentioned. Check.
- The reading is a story: "ten rials" asked and "eight rials" paid for a brass/copper lantern in Muttrah are invented, not real prices. Check that they are plausible or change them.
- Muttrah reading: "مَطْرَحَ" and "مَسْقَطَ" are vowelled as diptotes (no tanwin, -a in the genitive). Check.
- Facts to verify: Khan el-Khalili grew up around a medieval khan (14th century); Souq al-Hamidiyya has a high arched metal roof; the coppersmiths' square in Fez (Seffarine, not named in the text); Muttrah Souq near the harbour.
- Opening hours and Friday: written deliberately vaguely (varies by country and shop; some close for Friday prayer or the morning; some close in the early afternoon in hot places). Check that nothing is over-generalised.
- Tea: "accepting does not oblige you to buy" - check that this matches common etiquette across the countries mentioned; Moroccan mint tea is mentioned as a regional example.
- Bargaining section: says bread, vegetables and many everyday goods usually have a set price; check.
- Photos: souq-4 (Muttrah) shows a souvenir shop; the caption avoids saying whether prices are labelled.
