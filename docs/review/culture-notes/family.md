# Culture chapter 9 "Family, names and greetings" (cul.family) - open points for a teacher

- Kunya: text says "traditionally the eldest son; some families use the eldest child, son or daughter". Check wording and regional practice (e.g. kunya after an eldest daughter when there is no son).
- "Some men carry a traditional kunya even before they have children" (e.g. Ali -> Abu Hassan): kept vague on purpose; confirm.
- Ibn / bin: "in the Gulf the word often appears as bin in men's full names" and "identity cards list first, father's, grandfather's and family name": check that this is a fair general statement.
- "In most Arab countries a woman keeps her own family name when she marries": confirm (believed true; practice differs in e.g. Lebanon/Tunisia socially).
- Friday "a weekly day off in many Arab countries": deliberately not "all" (UAE moved to a Sat-Sun weekend in 2022).
- Handshake/kissing and "the person who arrives greets those already there": general etiquette, regional variation.
- Layla "usually linked with layl, night": etymology is the popular explanation; confirm wording.
- Nur "given to both boys and girls": confirm.
- ustadh as polite "Mr" for an educated man "in Egypt and the Levant": confirm regions.
- fams12 "صَبَاحُ الْخَيْر يَا أُسْتَاذ سَامِي" uses the course's pause style after yā (formal MSA: يَا أُسْتَاذُ سَامِي).
- fams7 uses وَالِدُ (father, parent) to avoid "أَبُو سَامِي هُوَ أَبُو سَامِي"; check that a level-2 learner gets it (glossed).
- Reading: "اِبْنُهُ الْأَكْبَر" (his eldest son) uses akbar, a level-6 course word, glossed in segs.
