# Open points for a teacher: cul.calligraphy (n 3, level 2)

1. Digits by region: the chapter says Arabic-Indic digits are "often" used in Egypt, Iraq, the Gulf and much of the
   eastern Arab world, and 0-9 "mostly" in Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia. Usage is mixed (Levant, Gulf signage,
   phones), so please check the wording.
2. Names of the digits: 0-9 called "al-arqam al-arabiyya" in the Maghreb and ٠١٢٣ "al-arqam al-hindiyya" in the
   east. This is the common naming, but some speakers use the names the other way round. Please confirm.
3. Ruq'a as everyday handwriting "especially in the eastern Arab world, learnt at school". Maghrebi handwriting
   differs. Please check how strong this claim should be.
4. Diwani "grew up at the Ottoman court"; Kufic "named after Kufa"; Naskh "the base of most print"; Thuluth "on
   mosques, gates and book titles". These are the standard textbook summaries. Please confirm they are not
   oversimplified.
5. Ibn Muqla (10th century, Baghdad) linked with the proportion system based on rhombic dots and the alif. The
   ijaza (licence) is described for the Ottoman tradition only.
6. Coins: "from the end of the 7th century many coins carried only writing" refers to the Umayyad coin reform under
   Abd al-Malik (about 696-697). No date is given in the text.
7. Languages: "Kurdish in parts of Iraq and Iran" (Sorani) and "Uyghur in western China". Turkish switched to Latin
   letters in 1928.
8. Language: "فِي الْعَرَبِيَّةِ ثَمَانِيَةٌ وَعِشْرُونَ حَرْفًا" is used for "Arabic has 28 letters". Some teachers might prefer
   "لِلْعَرَبِيَّةِ" or "فِي اللُّغَةِ الْعَرَبِيَّةِ". The cals2 mistake uses the plural حُرُوف (in the course glossary, not a
   token in the chapter).
9. Pause forms follow the day files: the last word of a sentence has no case ending (e.g. جَمِيل, الْيَسَار).
10. Hero photo: the Commons description says the pens are bamboo. The text says reed, and quiz calq4 avoids
    "bamboo" as an option.
11. Section 2 photo: a Kufic folio from Walters MS W.552, a Qur'an fragment. The caption only says "a page in Kufic
    script on parchment".
