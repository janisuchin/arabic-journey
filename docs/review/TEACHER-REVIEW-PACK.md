# Arabic Journey: review pack for an Arabic teacher

Thank you for reviewing. **Arabic Journey** is a 90-day self-study course in Modern Standard Arabic (fuṣḥā) for
English-speaking adult beginners. Three rounds of AI review have checked it (about 900 findings, almost all
fixed). A qualified human teacher is the final check: nothing in the course is marked "approved" until you have reviewed it.

## 1. Open the app

- **Review edition (every lesson open): https://ar-journey-unlocked.german-journey.workers.dev**
- Learner edition (days open one by one): https://ar-journey.german-journey.workers.dev

It runs in any browser, on a phone or a computer. No account is needed. Progress is saved only in your browser.

Before you start, please know:
- **Tap any Arabic word** to see its meaning and reading. The meaning shown is the one for that sentence, so a word
  can show different meanings in different lessons.
- The line marked **⟶** under the Arabic is the transliteration. The app writes it automatically (ALA-LC style:
  ā ī ū, ḥ ṣ ḍ ṭ ẓ, ʿ ʾ). You can turn it off, or turn off the vowel marks, in **Settings**.
- **The audio is the device's own text-to-speech voice, not a recording.** Please judge the text, not the voice.
- **House style** (chosen on purpose; tell us if you disagree): full vowel marks on every word, but most mid-sentence
  nouns are shown in pause form with no case ending (مَكْتَب كَبِير). Endings are shown mainly where a lesson teaches them
  (for example the object -an, the dual, the sound plural). Section 4 (R12-47) asks whether this policy is right. يَا أُسْتَاذ is written without ḍamma.
  بِخَيْر is read bi-khayr.

## 2. What we ask

1. **Correct Arabic**: spelling, vowel marks, grammar, agreement, and answer keys in quizzes and tests.
2. **Natural fuṣḥā**: words or phrases that are colloquial, old-fashioned or unnatural for a learner of MSA.
3. **Teaching notes**: grammar explanations that are wrong, or simplified so far that they mislead.
4. **Culture facts**: the Explore chapters (coffee, family, food, Ramadan, places, souq, desert, calligraphy,
   science) and the culture notes under each dialogue.
5. **The questions below**: the AI reviewers raised these and left them for a human to decide.

A suggested order if time is short (about 3 hours): days 1–10 (the foundations), then one day from each later week,
the grammar cards, two role-plays (Scenarios), and the week tests.

**How to report.** A list is enough: *where* (e.g. "Day 26, dialogue, line 15"), *what is wrong*, *your
correction*. For the questions below, reply with the number and **Keep**, **Change** (with your fix) or a comment.

### Grammar rules and simplifications in teaching notes

- **R12-23** **Day 37 mistake card (sentence 1); week 6 test, question 3** — The card says "Right before a past verb, مَا means 'not'". The test "Fill the gap: 'What did you do on Friday?'" offers مَاذَا (marked correct) and مَا (marked wrong). But مَا فَعَلْتَ؟ is also good MSA for "what did you do?". — Reword the card to "use مَاذَا to avoid confusion", and remove مَا from the test options?
- **R12-24** **Day 42 mistake card (sentence 2)** — رَكِبَ جُحَا عَلَى الْحِمَار is marked wrong. رَكِبَ عَلَى is attested and common in modern writing; transitive رَكِبَ is the classical preference. — Present it as "better" rather than "wrong"?
- **R12-25** **Day 82 mistake card (sentence 4)** — مُرْفَق مَعَ هٰذِهِ الرِّسَالَة سِيرَتِي الذَّاتِيَّة is marked wrong, and مُرْفَقَة right. Agreement with the nearest noun favours مُرْفَقَة, but frozen masculine مُرْفَق is very common in real letters. — Is "wrong" too strong?
- **R12-26** **Day 75 (sentence 4, line 9); grammar card "qāla + inna"** — Reported yes/no questions are taught as سَأَلَنِي هَلْ فَهِمْتُ. This is common in the press; careful style prefers سَأَلَنِي إِنْ كُنْتُ / عَمَّا إِذَا كُنْتُ. — Keep هَلْ as the taught form, or add a remark?
- **R12-27** **Day 77 line 8; grammar card "man = whoever"** — مَنْ يَعْمَلُ كَثِيرًا يَجِدُ النَّجَاح (indicative) is offered as a paraphrase of the proverb مَنْ جَدَّ وَجَدَ. As a conditional it would be jussive: مَنْ يَعْمَلْ … يَجِدِ النَّجَاحَ. The indicative works only as a relative ("the one who"). — Keep, or switch to the jussive?
- **R12-28** **Day 80, sentence 3** — إِنْ دَرَسْتَ كُلَّ يَوْمٍ، نَجَحْتَ is the classical past–past conditional. It is correct but sounds archaic next to the course's own فَسَـ pattern. — Change it to فَسَتَنْجَحُ?
- **R12-29** **Day 66 (sentence 4, objective 4); week 10 test, question 9** — "I'll call you tomorrow" is taught as أَتَّصِلُ بِكَ غَدًا (plain present). The lesson promises "promise to call back", and the future with سَـ is taught in week 8. — Use سَأَتَّصِلُ بِكَ?
- **R12-30** **Grammar card "nisba"** — The card says "Drop a final ā or the article first: لُبْنَان → لُبْنَانِيّ", but nothing is dropped in that example. Better examples: أَمْرِيكَا → أَمْرِيكِيّ, الْأُرْدُنّ → أُرْدُنِيّ. — Confirm the example.
- **R12-31** **Grammar card "accusative"** — The card says that at the end of a sentence "the ending is often not pronounced". In pause, -an is pronounced -ā (khubzā); only -un and -in drop. — Correct the wording?
- **R12-32** **Grammar card "family iḍāfa"** — The card says أَخ / أَب "become أَخُو and أَبُو before another word". That is only the nominative; the other cases are أَخَا / أَخِي and أَبَا / أَبِي. — Add "as the subject"?
- **R12-33** **Day 6 quiz, question 4; Day 9 quiz, question 6** — The quizzes say the l of the article is "silent / not pronounced" before a sun letter. It actually assimilates, and the sun letter is doubled; the pronunciation page now says this correctly. — Should the quizzes say "blends into the next letter"?
- **R12-34** **Day 15, sentence 4 (mistake card)** — The card explains "آ (a + hamza) at the start means 'I'". The order is reversed: آ is hamza + long ā. — Fix the wording?
- **R12-35** **Day 88 intro** — The hamza-seat rule (kasra → yāʾ, ḍamma → wāw, otherwise alif) leaves out initial hamza, long vowels and hamza on the line (شَيْء). — Is that enough at this level, or should "usually" be added?
- **R12-36** **Letter page "alif"** — "At the start of a word it carries a hamza" ignores hamzat al-waṣl (الـ, اِسْم), which the course teaches later. — Add "usually"?
- **R12-37** **Day 77, line 7** — بَلْ تَعْنِي: كُلُّ شَخْص: as the object of تَعْنِي it would be كُلَّ شَخْصٍ. The colon allows a quotation reading. — Keep the nominative?
- **R12-38** **Day 42, line 10** — حَمَلَا الْحِمَار عَلَى ظَهْرَيْهِمَا uses the dual. Classical style puts a pair's single body parts in the plural: ظُهُورِهِمَا. — Keep the dual?
- **R12-39** **Day 56, sentence 6** — الْعَاشِرُ مِنْ دِيسَمْبِر يَوْمُ أَرْبِعَاء has no article, while the rest of the course (and this lesson's line 2) writes يَوْمُ الْأَرْبِعَاءِ. — Add the article?
- **R12-40** **Holiday-story scenario, line 3; Cinema scenario, line 5** — Huda asks the couple كَيْفَ سَافَرْتُمَا؟ and then switches to the singular لَمْ تَذْهَبْ. In the cinema, the seller uses the dual تُرِيدَانِ أَنْ تَجْلِسَا to Daniel, who is alone at the window but buying two tickets. Both are defensible, but they may confuse learners. — Keep or adjust?

### Pronunciation descriptions

- **R12-41** **Pronunciation pages "emphatics", "sīn–ṣād", "ḍād"** — The darkened vowel after an emphatic is compared to the "u in but", the vowel in "sun" and the "du of dull". The backed a is nearer the a of "father" or the o of "hot". — Which English anchor should be used?
- **R12-42** **Letter "ghayn"; pronunciation page "ʿayn and ghayn"** — The pages compare غ to the French r and place it "near the uvula". MSA غ is velar (the voiced partner of خ, as the ghayn page itself says); the French r is uvular. — Is this acceptable as an approximation?
- **R12-43** **Pronunciation page "tāʾ and ṭāʾ"** — "Neither one has the puff of air of an English t": MSA ت is usually described as lightly aspirated and ط as unaspirated. — Should it say only "ṭ has no puff of air"?
- **R12-44** **Pronunciation page "long vowels"** — "seat" vs "sit" differ in vowel quality as well as length. The page also says "Many job words have one: طَالِب", but a student is not a job. — Reword?
- **R12-45** **Pronunciation page "dāl–dhāl" (common error)** — The page says Egyptians say هٰذَا as "hāda / hāza". Everyday Egyptian is "da", and the d/z point fits other words better. — Adjust the example?

### Translation nuance

- **R12-46** **Day 50 lines 7, 11, 14; Day 52 line 8; Day 54 line 3; Day 57 line 4; Day 70 line 5; vocabulary "kātib" example** — The English adds or shifts meaning that is not in the Arabic: "busy **all day** on Saturday", "**very** crowded", "See you **on Sunday**!" for إِلَى اللِّقَاء, "the **other** students in **our** class", "**you know**", "your passport**s**" for singular جَوَازَ السَّفَر, "formal **emails**" for رَسَائِل رَسْمِيَّة, and "He **was** the first Arab writer" for هُوَ أَوَّلُ كَاتِبٍ. — Keep free renderings, or make the English closer to the Arabic for learners who compare word by word?

### Spelling and vocalisation house style

- **R12-47** **Whole course: case endings mid-sentence** — Indefinite nouns in ة never get tanwīn, even as objects, after kāna or as adverbs: مَرَّة وَاحِدَة (day 59, against مَرَّةً وَاحِدَةً on day 53), قَصِيدَة لَهُ, لَيْلَة وَاحِدَة. Many mid-phrase nouns are left in pause form (مَكْتَب كَبِير, أَقَارِب كَثِيرُونَ, خَمْس جُمَل, يَوْم مِثْل هٰذَا, نُفَايَات), while other sentences carry full endings. Round 3 flags single cases (#20, #34, #42); this is the general policy question. — Confirm the pause convention, and decide where endings must be shown (adverbial -an, kāna predicates, counted nouns)?
- **R12-48** **Day 82 vs Day 84** — The letter opening is تَحِيَّة طَيِّبَة وَبَعْدُ (pause) on day 82 but تَحِيَّةً طَيِّبَةً وَبَعْدُ on day 84. The accusative is what real letters show. — Use one form on both days?
- **R12-49** **Day 18 and other places (the name ʿAlī)** — عَلِي is written without the final shadda throughout; the standard spelling is عَلِيّ. — Normalise?
- **R12-50** **Day 21, Day 31 and elsewhere** — إِذَنْ is used consistently; إِذًا is the more common modern spelling. — Keep it or switch?
- **R12-51** **Culture: Science** — الْخُوَارِزْمِيُّ is vocalised khuwārizmī, while Arabic sources often give الخَوارزمي and the English text writes al-Khwārizmī. — Which vocalisation?
- **R12-52** **Day 6 line 9; Day 28 line 1; Culture: Souq; Lost-phone scenario, line 4; vocabulary "sūq" (gender: m)** — سُوق is treated as masculine everywhere (سُوق كَبِير, سُوقٌ قَدِيمٌ, السُّوق مُزْدَحِم). Many references treat it as feminine; both genders are attested. — Keep it masculine, or teach the feminine?
- **R12-53** **Day 25 culture note; Day 57 culture note; vocabulary "munākh" note** — Phrases are quoted in their genitive or accusative form out of context: الطَّابِقِ الثَّانِي, الْمُسَافِرِينَ (as heard in announcements), تَغَيُّرِ الْمُنَاخ. — Give citation forms (الطَّابِقُ, الْمُسَافِرُونَ, تَغَيُّرُ), or explain the form?
