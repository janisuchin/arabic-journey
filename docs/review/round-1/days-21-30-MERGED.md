# days-21-30: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked every Arabic string in days 21–30 (titles, intros, sentences and their mistakes, dialogues, readings, quizzes) for spelling, harakat, case endings, agreement, verb forms, translations, literals, seg and tap glosses, answer keys and the generated transliteration. Reviewer B: I read the whole export of days 21–30 (2,685 lines: intros, objectives, sentences with mistakes, dialogues, readings, quizzes and all seg/tap glosses) and checked every Arabic string for spelling, harakat, case endings (numbers 11–99 + -an, 3–10 + plural, 100/1000 + singular, genitive after prepositions and place words, accusative after لٰكِنَّ and أَنْ), agreement (non-human plurals as feminine singular, verb before plural subject, ordinals), verb forms (dual imperatives تَفَضَّلَا/تَعَالَيَا/اُنْظُرَا, jussive لَا تَقْلَقْ, subjunctive أَنْ أَشْتَرِيَ), the English translations and literals, the transliteration, and every answer key. The Arabic in this chunk is in very good shape: I found no spelling, harakat, case or agreement errors in the Arabic itself and all 70 quiz answer keys are correct. The 4 errors are: one major gloss error (تَذْهَبُ tapped as 'you go (to a man)' where it is 'Layla goes', including on the marked correct quiz answer in day 22), one minor gloss error (يَنْزِلُ glossed 'falls, comes down' while the lesson and quiz teach 'to get off'), one minor title translation (أَوْ rendered 'and' in day 24), and one systematic engine issue (a standalone ة in prose rendered as 'A / at', 9 occurrences across days 23–30). The notes cover gloss-wording consistency, a citation-form quibble, the ج pronunciation in Cairo, and the engine's identical transliteration of deliberately misspelt forms.
