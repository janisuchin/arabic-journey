# weeks-tests: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked all 13 weekly tests (128 items), the week titles, topics and level table in audit/weeks-tests.txt: every Arabic string and its harakat, case endings, agreement, verb forms and moods, the English prompts and options, every tap gloss and every answer key, and the transliteration against the Arabic. The Arabic itself is clean: every answer key is correct and unique (one debatable case, مَا vs مَاذَا in w6t3, listed as a note), case endings after إِنَّ / لَيْسَ / كَانَ / أَنْ / لَنْ / لَمْ and prepositions are right, agreement (feminine أُذُن, non-human plurals with f. sg.) is right. The errors are all in the tap-gloss layer and the transliteration engine: 3 major glosses that contradict the test itself (دَرَّسَ=lesson on the right answer of w10t7, شَرِبْتَ=I drank in w13t8, مَا=what on the negation in w6t4), 7 minor gloss mislabels (wrong mood/particle labels, a passive label on an active verb, a title gloss carrying the compound's meaning), and 3 engine findings: the relative pronouns الَّذِي / الَّتِي / الَّذِينَ transliterated Illadhī, the الِامْتِحَان case rendered liāmtiḥān, and the known الشَّاي quirk. Pattern: dictionary glosses are shared across contexts, so a word's gloss written for one sentence shows up wrongly in another (مَا, السَّفَر, الشَّرَاب, حَارّ), and distractor glosses often reveal the answer.

Reviewer B: I read all 2,055 lines of audit/weeks-tests.txt (13 week titles with segs, 128 test items with options, answer keys, taps and transliterations, and the 13 level rows) against the brief. Every answer key is correct and no option set has a second correct answer; the Arabic strings themselves (harakat, agreement, moods after lam/lan/an, kāna/laysa accusatives, dual and plural forms, passives) are sound. The errors are concentrated in the tap glosses, which are drawn from a global word list and sometimes carry a sense or a grammatical tag from another sentence: three of them directly contradict the marked answer (مَا 'what' in مَا سَبَحْتُ, دَرَّسَ 'lesson', شَرِبْتَ 'I drank') and are major; six more are misleading tags ('after an' on indicative forms, 'after lam' on a lā-prohibitive, 'wrong after an' on a sentence with no an, 'bigger' for a superlative, 'syrup', 'is opened'). Two engine faults are major because they teach a wrong pronunciation: the relative pronouns الَّذِي/الَّذِينَ/الَّتِي come out as 'Illadhī…' and الِامْتِحَان as 'liāmtiḥān'; the الشَّاي → 'shāī' quirk is noted once. Total 14 errors (5 major, 9 minor) and 12 notes, mostly on inconsistent mid-sentence case endings, wa- hyphenation and small gloss wording.
