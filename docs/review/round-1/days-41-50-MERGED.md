# days-41-50: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Read the whole export for days 41-50 (2,733 lines: titles, intros, objectives, sentences with mistakes, dialogues, readings, quizzes, completions) and checked every Arabic string for spelling, harakat, case, agreement and verb mood (لَمْ jussive, أَنْ subjunctive, imperatives, prohibitions, faʿlān feminines, compound numbers), the English and literal translations, every seg and tap gloss, every answer key and the transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: I found no misspelt word, wrong case ending, agreement error, wrong verb form or wrong answer key in the ten days. All 14 errors are in the gloss/transliteration layer: 3 major tap glosses that teach the wrong meaning (سَلَامَتُكِ labelled 'to a man' in day43 and day46, تَفْتَحُ glossed as passive 'is opened' in day44, negative مَا glossed 'what' in day41), 8 minor glosses where a dictionary entry does not fit the sentence it is tapped in (imperatives glossed as 3rd-person present, 'after an'/'after lam' labels on the wrong forms, فِي الْيَوْم 'today', vocalised أَنْتِ described as unvocalised), 1 minor grammar-fact (day41 culture gives colloquial salāmtik for the feminine, contradicting day43), and 3 engine issues (bare ة transliterated as 'A / at', proclitic+name capitalised as one word 'Wasāmī', شَاي as 'Shāī'). The recurring pattern is that tap glosses come from a single dictionary sense and are reused in sentences where another sense or form is meant, and that the dictionary glosses of the 'wrong' forms in mistakes blocks were copied from the 'right' forms. Notes cover register (تَعْبَان, لَازِم), two small additions in English translations, and transliteration limits that hide a contrast (ثَلَاثَة vs ثَلَاثَ).

Reviewer B: Checked every Arabic string in days 41-50 (titles, intros, sentences with their mistakes, dialogues, readings, quizzes, culture notes) for harakat, case endings, jussive after لَمْ, subjunctive after أَنْ, imperatives and negative imperatives, dual forms, feminine body parts, faʿlān/faʿlā adjectives, numbers 3-10 and 20-99, plus every seg and tap gloss, every ⟶ transliteration and every answer key. The core Arabic of the lessons is sound: no misspelt word, wrong verb form or wrong answer key was found, and the grammar rules taught (لَمْ + jussive, verb before plural subject stays singular, أَنْ + -a, ʿaṭshān → ʿaṭshā, 3-10 opposite gender) are correct. All 13 findings are in the tap-gloss dictionary and the transliteration engine rather than in the lessons themselves: 4 major gloss errors where a tapped word shows the wrong sense or wrong gender/tense (سَلَامَتُكِ labelled 'to a man', تَفْتَحُ glossed as passive, مَا glossed 'what' in a negation lesson, يَحْضُرُ glossed as past), 9 minor items (ة rendered 'A / at', 'Wasāmī'-style capitalisation of names after wa-/li-, 'Shāī' for shāy, garbled imperative glosses, glosses copied from another lesson's sense, and an imperative rule in the day 44 intro that omits the helping alif). The pattern is that the one-word tap dictionary reuses a single gloss across all senses and tenses of a word, so each new grammar point (negation مَا, jussive vs indicative, imperative vs statement) inherits glosses from the lesson where the word first appeared. Notes cover register (تَعْبَان, salāmtik, لَازِم), one over-strict 'mistake' (رَكِبَ عَلَى), and small English additions.
