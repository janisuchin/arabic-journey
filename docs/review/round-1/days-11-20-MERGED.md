# days-11-20: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Read the whole export for days 11–20 (2,866 lines): every Arabic string with its harakat, the English and literal translations, all seg and tap glosses, all quiz answer keys and the mistakes right/wrong pairs, plus the generated transliteration. The Arabic itself is in very good shape: verb conjugations (singular, plural, dual, feminine -īna), colour agreement (aḥmar/ḥamrāʾ), object -an including the tāʾ marbūṭa case, number–noun gender polarity (عَشْر كَلِمَات vs عَشَرَة أَرْغِفَة, سَبْعَ مَلَاعِق vs سَبْعَة صُحُون), broken plurals with feminine-singular agreement, dual -āni/-ayni and هِيَ for non-human plurals are all correct, and every quiz answer key checked out. I found 4 errors: 1 major grammar-fact (the repeated rule that -īna / -ayni appear 'after a verb', which is wrong for a verb's subject and is contradicted by the course's own VSO sentences; should be 'as the object of a verb'), 1 minor gloss (حَارّ glossed 'hot (weather)' in the spicy-food lesson quiz), and 2 minor engine issues (الشَّاي → 'shāī'; capitalised fused prefixes on names such as 'Wadānyāl', 'Lidānyāl'). Eight notes cover orthographic and style points (tanwīn placement on مَقْهىً, عَلِي without shadda, hyphenation inconsistency, clumsy plural glosses, pausal spellings).

Reviewer B: Checked every Arabic string in days 11-20 (sentences, mistakes pairs, dialogues, readings, titles, culture notes, all quiz prompts, options and answer keys) for harakat, case endings, verb conjugation (present-tense prefixes and the -īna/-ūna/-āni/-ayni endings), gender/number agreement including the feminine-singular rule for plurals of things and the gender polarity of numbers 3-10 (سَبْعَة صُحُون, سَبْعَ مَلَاعِق, عَشَرَة أَرْغِفَة, عَشْر كَلِمَات, ثَلَاثَة أَكْوَاب all correct), the sun/moon assimilation, every seg and tap gloss, the English translations and literals, and the transliteration against the Arabic. The chunk is clean: no major errors, no wrong answer key, no wrong verb form or agreement anywhere. Five minor findings: one orthographic (مَقْهىً should be written مَقْهًى), one tap gloss out of sense (حَارّ glossed 'hot (weather)' in the tastes lesson), and three systematic engine issues (الشَّاي rendered 'shāī' unless a sukūn is written; proclitic + name capitalised as 'Wadānyāl'/'Lisāmī'; a bare ة in English prose rendered 'A / at', which makes several grammar explanations unreadable in the romanised view). Six notes on register and consistency (هَلْ … أَمْ, indicative used for 'let's', عَلِي without shadda, two gloss-label inconsistencies, حَبِيبِي).
