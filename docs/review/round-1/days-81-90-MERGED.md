# days-81-90: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: I read the whole export for days 81–90 (3,123 lines): every Arabic line with its harakat, case endings and agreement, every seg and tap gloss, every mistakes pair, every quiz key and the transliterations. The Arabic itself is in very good shape: I found no misspelt word, no wrong case ending, no wrong verb form and no wrong quiz answer across the ten days; the passive, إِذَا + past, أَنَّ/لٰكِنَّ + accusative, duals, non-human plural agreement and the formal-letter formulas are all correct. The 24 findings are 5 major and 19 minor. The real problems are in the shared tap glosses, which are reused from earlier lessons and sometimes carry the wrong sense or the wrong form into the new context: اِكْتَشَفَ glossed as 'was discovered' (defeating the active/passive contrast), قِيلَ glossed 'it is said' on a quiz distractor, عَبَّرَ glossed as an imperative, عُنْوَان glossed 'address' in the headline lesson, plus several minor sense mismatches (عَشَرَ 'part of fifteen', السَّاعَةَ 'at the hour', عَفْوًا 'you're welcome', و 'and' in a letter list). One mistakes pair (day 90, شَرِيكُ الْمُحَادَثَةِ) marks a correct, common phrase as wrong. The transliteration engine has one systematic fault worth fixing: الِاحْتِرَام / الِاسْتِمَاع / الِامْتِحَان come out as 'liāḥtirām / liāstimāʿ / Iliāmtiḥān', which affects the title of day 89 and the closing formula of day 82; smaller engine quirks (Wabiālʾiḍāfati, maqhanā, Illatī, shāī/wāū, ashalu, raʾī, 'A / at', 'Wasāmī') are reported once each with examples. Ten notes cover register, consistency and story logic.

Reviewer B: I read all 3,123 lines of audit/days-81-90.txt (days 81-90: passive, formal letter, debate connectors, Week 12 review, listening/reading/speaking/writing reviews, final exam, next steps) and checked every Arabic string, its harakat and case endings, agreement, verb forms, translations and literals, seg and tap glosses, quiz answer keys and the transliteration against the Arabic. The Arabic itself is clean: I found no misspelt word, wrong case ending, wrong agreement, wrong mood or wrong answer key in the sentences, dialogues, readings or quizzes. The 16 reported errors are 3 major gloss errors (اِكْتَشَفَ glossed as passive 'was discovered'; قِيلَ glossed 'it is said' on a distractor whose prompt is 'it is said'; عَبَّرَ glossed as an imperative and the neighbouring past verbs glossed in the present), 5 minor gloss/answer-key issues (shared tap glosses pulled from other sentences: 'ten (part of fifteen)', 'at the hour', 'the address', 'you're welcome'; a tap on the wrong form claiming '-a after an'; a grammatical iḍāfa marked 'wrong'), and 7 engine findings, of which 3 are major because they produce non-words the learner will read aloud: the hamzat-waṣl maṣdars after al- (الِاسْتِمَاع/الِامْتِحَان/الِاحْتِرَام → 'liāstimāʿ', 'Iliāmtiḥān', 'liāḥtirām' in titles and formulas of days 82, 84, 85, 87, 89), standalone الَّذِي/الَّتِي → 'Illadhī/Illatī', and وَبِالْإِضَافَةِ → 'Wabiālʾiḍāfati'; the minor ones are the known bare-letter and وَ-name quirks, final -āy/-āw rendered as vowels, and 'ashalu' for أَسْهَلُ. Pattern: the content author's segs are consistently right while the shared tap vocabulary occasionally carries a sense from another day; and the transliteration engine mishandles every word whose hamzat waṣl is written with a kasra under the alif of al-.
