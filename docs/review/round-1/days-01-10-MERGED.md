# days-01-10: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Read all 2,490 lines of audit/days-01-10.txt (days 1–10: titles, intros, objectives, real-life stories, sentences with literals and mistakes, dialogues, readings, quizzes and completions). Checked every Arabic string for spelling, harakat, case endings, hamza seats, tāʾ marbūṭa, agreement (gender, number, definiteness), verb forms and imperatives, iḍāfa and number constructions, the English translations and literals, every seg and tap gloss, every quiz answer key and the generated transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: I found no misspelt word, no agreement or case error, no wrong verb form and no wrong quiz answer key; number phrases (ثَلَاثَة إِخْوَة, عَشَرَة أَسْئِلَة, سَبْعَة أَيَّام), time expressions (إِلَّا رُبْعًا), iḍāfa forms and the imperatives (اِشْتَرِ, اُدْرُسْ, اِفْتَحُوا, تَعَالَوْا) are all correct MSA. The errors are all in the tap-gloss layer: one major (مُدَرِّسَة glossed 'school' on the correct answer of d8q2) and three minor dictionary glosses that show the wrong sense in context (ل and و glossed as the preposition/conjunction inside letter lists and a letter quiz; أَنْتَ/أَنْتِ glossed as 'written without vowel marks' although fully vocalised, in nine places), plus one minor engine issue (capitalised 'Walubnān Wamiṣr' after the clitic وَ). Eleven notes cover register (تَعْبَان), small translation drift (really / my bag / our class), two culture facts (US week start, Levant digits), the 'silent lām' simplification and transliteration readability. Totals: 1 major, 4 minor, 11 notes.

Reviewer B: Read the whole export of days 1–10 (2,490 lines): every Arabic string with its harakat and case endings, agreement, verb forms, the English and literal translations, every seg and tap gloss, every quiz answer key and the generated transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: iḍāfa endings (كِتَابُ دَانْيَال, سَيَّارَةُ الْأُسْتَاذ, مُدِيرِ الْمَدْرَسَة), the five-noun forms أَبُوكَ / أَبِيكَ / أَخُوكَ, number–noun agreement (ثَلَاثَة إِخْوَة, عَشَرَة أَسْئِلَة), waṣla handling (لٰكِنِ الْحَمْدُ, هَلِ الْيَوْم, كَمِ السَّاعَة), the imperatives (اُدْرُسْ, اِفْتَحُوا, اِجْلِسْ, اِشْتَرِ) and إِلَّا رُبْعًا are all correct, and all 62 quiz answer keys check out. I found 6 errors: 1 major (the tap gloss of مُدَرِّسَة says 'school' on the correct answer of the day-8 teacher quiz) and 5 minor (the hedged tap gloss for the fully vocalised أَنْتَ / أَنْتِ; the letters ل and و glossed as the words 'for' and 'and' in letter lists; two English lines that add a possessive not in the Arabic; and the engine capitalising وَ before proper names as 'Walubnān Wamiṣr'). The pattern is that the dictionary-driven tap glosses, not the lesson content, are where the mistakes live. Eight notes cover register (تَعْبَان, مَصْر, سُوق gender), story logic, English intensifiers, engine capitalisation and the all-option-0 answer keys in week 1.
