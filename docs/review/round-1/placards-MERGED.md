# placards: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked all 20 placards (alphabet, vowel marks, sun/moon, greetings, polite words, pronouns, attached pronouns, numbers 0-10, tens, weekdays, question words, colours, family, demonstratives, present and past tense, negation, prepositions, signs, café): every Arabic string with its harakat, every table cell, seg, tap gloss, English gloss and the generated transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: no spelling, harakat, case, agreement or verb-form errors were found, and all paradigms (present/past of كَتَبَ, possessive endings, colour feminines, numbers 3-10 with plural, diptote حَمْرَاءُ) are correct. The 10 findings are all in the tap-gloss layer and the transliteration engine: 4 major glosses where a consonant-skeleton match shows a homograph or another lesson's sense (verb stems glossed 'was written', past ـُوا glossed as an imperative ending, negative مَا glossed 'what', أَحَد glossed with a quiz-distractor text), 2 minor glosses (و as 'and' among the long-vowel letters, short 'ba/bu/bi' glosses on the long-vowel items), and 4 one-time engine quirks (bare ة/letters transliterated by sound in prose, شَاي as shāī, الِاسْتِفْهَام as liāstifhām, capitalised embedded fragments). 9 notes for the owner cover wording and labelling choices.

Reviewer B: Checked all 20 placard sets in audit/placards.txt end to end: every Arabic item, table cell (alphabet, sun/moon), example sentence, harakat, seg and tap gloss, and the generated transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: the alphabet and sun/moon tables are complete and correct, the pronoun, possessive, number, weekday, colour, verb and negation paradigms are all right, and the example sentences carry correct case endings. Found 10 errors (4 major, 6 minor) and 9 notes. All four major findings are tap glosses, not Arabic: the consonant-skeleton matcher shows a homograph's meaning on the very word a note is explaining (كتب stems glossed 'was written', ـُوا glossed as the imperative plural, مَا negative glossed 'what', أَحَد glossed with a quiz 'wrong form' message). The minor findings are gloss inconsistencies (possessive endings described as 'object endings', long-vowel segs written as short) and three engine quirks (bare letters in prose transliterated by their sound cell, شَاي → Shāī, الِاسْتِفْهَامِ → liāstifhāmi), each reported once.
