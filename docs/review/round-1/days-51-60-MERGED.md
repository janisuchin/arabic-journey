# days-51-60: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked every Arabic string in days 51–60 (titles, intro and objective examples, 5–6 model sentences with their mistakes pairs, 14–16 dialogue lines, readings, all quiz Arabic and answer keys) for harakat, case endings (predicates of كَانَ / يَكُونُ / لَيْسَ, subjunctive after أَنْ / لَنْ / لِـ, jussive after لَمْ and in negative imperatives, duals after prepositions, numbers 3–10 and 11–99 with their counted nouns, ordinals in dates), agreement, verb forms (hollow verbs زَارَ / كَانَ / عَادَ / ضَاعَ, imperatives incl. dual ضَعَا and plural تَنْسَوْا), translations and literals, every seg and tap gloss, and the transliteration. The Arabic itself is clean: I found no misspelling, wrong case ending, wrong mood or agreement error, and all 70 quiz answer keys and all mistakes right/wrong pairs are correct; the grammar facts (lan + -a, li- purpose, yakūnu + -an, numbers 3–10 reverse gender, ordinal dates with min, dual -ayni after li-, hollow-verb shortening, -nī vs -ī) are all right. The 9 errors are all minor and all in the glossing/engine layer: six gloss problems where the shared tap dictionary gives the wrong person (تَأْتِيَ 'you come' for Layla), wrong gender (سَلَامَتُكِ 'to a man'), a misleading sense (الْحَرَارَة 'fever'), a wrong grammatical label (بَارِدًا 'object'), 'wrong after an/lam' annotations leaking onto correct standalone quiz options, and a false 'written without vowel marks' note on a vocalised أَنْتَ; plus the three known engine quirks (bare letters ة / و in prose, names after وَ / لِ capitalised with the clitic, الشَّاي → sh-shāī). Notes cover register (لَازِم), one indefinite day name (يَوْمُ أَرْبِعَاء), small English additions and English annotations embedded in Arabic 'wrong' fields.

Reviewer B: Checked every Arabic string, harakat, case ending, verb mood, agreement, English translation and literal, seg and tap gloss, answer key and transliteration in days 51–60 (weekend plans with لَنْ, invitations with لِـ, weather, seasons with يَكُونُ, months, dates with ordinals, airport, hotel, booking with hollow verbs, lost wallet with object pronouns). The Arabic itself is in very good shape: subjunctives after أَنْ/لَنْ/لِـ, jussives after لَمْ and in prohibitions, hollow-verb shortening, kāna/yakūnu predicates in the accusative, number–noun gender rules (خَمْسَ دَرَجَات, سَبْعٌ وَعِشْرُونَ, خَمْسَةَ عَشَرَ ضَيْفًا), duals after prepositions and the diptotes (مَعَاطِفَ, حَمْرَاءَ) are all correct; every quiz answer key and every mistakes right/wrong pair is correct. 13 errors found: 2 major, both in the shared tap-gloss dictionary (لَيْلَى تَأْتِي glossed as '(they) come (f)'; سَلَامَتُكِ glossed 'to a man'), and 11 minor: five more tap-gloss leaks where a dictionary sense written for one context (the mistakes 'wrong after an/lam' notes, 'cold (object)', و='and', unvocalised أَنْتَ) shows on a word in a different context, one misplaced kasra on the waṣl alif of الِاسْتِقْبَال, and four engine quirks reported once each (ة→'A / at', Wadānyāl-type capitalisation, sh-shāī, no waṣla elision after tanwīn -an). The pattern to watch is the single tap dictionary: glosses tied to one sentence's context (person, 'wrong after…') surface on the same form elsewhere and mislead. 8 notes on paraphrase, register and conventions.
