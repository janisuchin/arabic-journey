# days-61-70: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked every Arabic string, harakat, case ending, agreement, verb form, translation, literal, seg and tap gloss, quiz answer key and transliteration in days 61-70 (comparative/superlative, yumkinu an, maṣdar, active participle, email formulas, verb forms, interview). The Arabic itself is clean: case endings after قَبْلَ/مِنْ with diptote comparatives (أَكْثَرَ), accusative apposition (أَشْهَرَ مَكَان), duals, subjunctives after أَنْ, feminine agreement and the superlative constructions are all correct, and every quiz answer key is right. 12 findings: 2 major, both tap glosses in day 68 that teach the wrong form of the very verbs the lesson is about (دَرَّسَ=lesson, اِسْتَعْمَلَ=use imperative); 10 minor: four context-free tap glosses that contradict the sentence sense (تَرُدَّ 'after lam', قَرِيبًا 'near', مُسَافِر 'passenger', root letter ل glossed as the preposition), one stray kasra on a hamzat waṣl mid-sentence (day 64 reading), and five engine quirks reported once each (clitic capitalisation Lisāmī/Walaylā, ة → 'A / at', الشَّاي → sh-shāī, bare letters in prose dropped or capitalised, وَاتَّصَلَ → wāttaṣala). Pattern: the content JSON is accurate; nearly all problems come from the shared tap dictionary serving one gloss to every sentence regardless of sense, and from the transliteration engine's handling of bare letters and clitics. Ten notes cover spelling consistency (أُرْدُنِيّ, الْإِنْجْلِيزِيَّة, الْإِلِكْتُرُونِيّ) and gloss polish.

Reviewer B: Read the whole export for days 61-70 (comparatives, superlatives, Petra, office, university, phone, email, verb patterns, language exchange, job interview): every Arabic line with its harakat, case endings and agreement, every verb form (subjunctive after أَنْ, imperatives, duals, hollow verbs), all English translations and literals, all seg and tap glosses, all 70 quiz answer keys and the mistake cards. The Arabic itself is clean: no wrong case after إِنَّ/لَيْسَ/كَانَ, correct diptote comparatives (قَبْلَ أَكْثَرَ مِنْ), correct numerals (عَشَرَةَ أَفْعَال, خَمْسِ سَنَوَات, ثَمَانِمِئَةِ دَرَجَة), correct feminine subjunctive تَقْرَئِيهَا, and all answer keys are right with no second correct option. I found 11 errors: 2 major, both tap glosses in day 68 that teach the wrong word (دَرَّسَ glossed 'lesson' and اِسْتَعْمَلَ glossed as an imperative) in the very lesson about verb patterns; 9 minor: three missing iḍāfa vowels (مَدِينَة جِبَال, كَلِمَة دِيوَان, كَلِمَة مَدْرَسَة), five tap glosses that carry another sentence's sense (تَرُدَّ 'after lam', قَرِيبًا 'near', الْكِتَابَة 'spelling', مُسَافِر 'passenger', ل 'for', أَكْبَرُ 'bigger' in a superlative quiz), and two engine entries (the known ة / clitic-name / shāī quirks, plus 'wāttaṣala' and an empty '()' for a bare ئ). Pattern: the shared tap dictionary is the weak point; it resolves each Arabic word to one global gloss, so a word taught with a new sense (Form II verb, participle, superlative, 'soon') shows the older or a wrong meaning when tapped, while the per-sentence segs are almost always right. 7 notes for the owner on spelling consistency, tanwīn style and small gloss polish.
