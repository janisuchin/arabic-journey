# glossary-auto-1: round findings (merged A + B)

Reviewer A: Checked all ~1,700 auto-glossary entries (آتي … سيارتي) as spelling-level dictionary glosses that must hold wherever the unvocalised word appears. 20 major findings and 15 minor findings (several grouped). The dominant pattern is one-sidedness inherited from a single source sentence: (1) every تـ-prefixed present/future verb is glossed as either 'you (m)' or 'she' though the spelling is both; (2) every past ـت verb is glossed as only I, only you or only she; (3) ـك suffixes carry a fixed 'to a man / to a woman' tag; (4) Form II/III/V/VIII/X imperatives and 3ms past share a spelling but get one gloss, and Form IV 1s present vs 3ms past likewise; (5) prohibitions were stripped of لا and glossed as bare imperatives (تخف = 'be afraid'). Beyond these, 15 individual keys give a one-sentence sense while a basic beginner meaning of the same spelling is missing (رجل leg, جد grandfather, جمع plural, جملا sentences, الدين religion, العام year, درسنا we studied, الضيق narrow, الحساب account, حرب war, أحمل carry, أقدم I present, أنظف I clean), one wrong person (زارنا), one wrong leaked alternative (خالتك 'your cousin'), and ~70 glosses with sentence-specific syntax notes or contradictory quoted tenses leaked into the dictionary text.

Reviewer B: Checked all ~1,700 entries of glossary-auto-1 (آتي … سيارتي) as dictionary glosses for an unvocalised key that must hold for every vocalisation. 28 findings: 20 major, 8 minor (many grouped, so several hundred keys are affected). Three patterns dominate. (1) Homograph keys glossed with one reading only, where the omitted reading is basic or is itself taught in the course: المدرسة (school/teacher f), الرجل/رجل/رجلان (man/leg), العام (year), العالم (scholar), الدين (religion), حرب (war), جد (grandfather), جمع (plural), جملا (sentences), درسنا (we studied), المهمة (task), أقدم (I present), أحمل/حملت (carry). (2) Sentence-specific person leaked into verb glosses: every past ـت form is fixed to one of I/you/she; the تـ prefix is fixed to either 'you (m)' or 'she'; Form IV he-past vs I-present (أرسله) and ـنا 'we' vs 'us' (أخبرنا) are one-sided; Form II/IV/VIII/X past and imperative (اتصل, استعمل) are given as one or the other. (3) The most dangerous group: jussive forms after لا glossed as bare positive imperatives (تخف 'be afraid', تنس 'forget', تقلقي 'worry'), which teach the opposite of لا تَخَفْ 'don't be afraid', plus تأخذها given as an imperative; and the leaked contradiction خالتك ('your cousin'). Minor findings cover leaked case/mood notes ('after kāna', 'after an', '(object)'), gendered -k glosses fixed to 'to a woman', and quoted sentence translations ('I was afraid', 'good night'). Nouns, numbers, names, place names, months, colours and the ال‑ nouns are otherwise accurate; the second-sense omissions listed in notes are low risk.

**Errors: 42** (21 found by both reviewers, 21 by one), 25 major. Notes: 20.
